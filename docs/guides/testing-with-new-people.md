# Testing the app with people who have never seen it

Before the app goes to a new café (or after a big change), watch two or
three people who have never used it do real tasks, **with no help from
you**. It finds what confuses people far better than any review: every
place someone stops is a screen to make plainer.

## Who

- Two or three people who have **never seen the app**, ideally the kind of
  person who will use it: a new cashier, a barista, a shift manager.
- Not someone who helped build it or has been shown it.

## Before

1. Give each person their own login, with the role they would have.
2. Ask them to press **Practise first** on My account (or open
   **Practice**): everything they do happens in their own practice café,
   so nothing reaches the books.
3. Have a notebook and a timer. Sit beside them, where you can see the
   screen but not touch it.

## The five tasks

Read each task out, one at a time, in your own words. Do not say where to
tap. For a cashier or barista:

1. "A customer wants two small lattes and pays by card."
2. "A customer buys an espresso and gives you 5,000 in cash."
3. "Table 1 sits down: take their order, then they pay later."
4. "The customer says one latte was wrong: give their money back for it."
5. "Your shift ends: count the drawer and close it."

For a manager: approve Omar's count, order from the supplier, receive the
delivery, send beans from the kitchen to the branch, and close the day. For
the accountant: record an expense, open three reports and say what they
show.

## While they work

- Say nothing, even when they are stuck. If they ask, say "What would you
  try?" Only step in after **two minutes** without progress, and note it.
- Note for each task: **how long** it took, **where they paused** or went
  back, what they **said** ("where is…?", "what does this mean?"), and any
  **mistake**.
- Ask them to think aloud: "say what you are looking for".

## After

1. For each task: done alone, done with a hint, or not done.
2. List every pause and mistake with its screen. The same one for two people
   is a screen to fix first.
3. Open **Reports → Where people get stuck**: their refusals and the help
   they opened are there too, under "In practice".
4. Fix the screen (fewer choices, plainer words, the next step shown), not
   the person: adding more help text is the last resort.
5. Test again with someone new after the fixes.

## The goal

A new cashier rings up correctly within **15 minutes**, alone. A new manager
gets through a normal day within **one shift**. When three people in a row
do the five tasks alone, the app is ready for that role.
